01 · Modular Design · 2025

overgrwth

A modular workspace system for creative spaces.

A creative's work is never over. There's always something to change, to add, or to remove.

overgrwth is built around that cycle. The whole system is two kinds of part: H-frames and modules. Every frame you add opens up new formations, and modules clip on only when you need them. Home or office, solo or studio, artist or architect, it grows with you, your space and what you make.

For
Designers, artists, content creators, engineers and architects
Focus
Space adaptability, physical and digital work in one place, keeping tools in reach

What I listened to while making overgrwth

A creative's work is never over. There's always something to change, to add, or to remove.

brief

Design a modular system for professional creative spaces that helps people manage their things and builds a connection with the workspace.

SpaceStudios change size, shape and use. The system has to fit whatever room it lands in.
Physical + digitalCreatives move between physical, digital and paper-based work all day. A workspace has to hold all three.
OrganisationTools, materials and references need a place close at hand, and that place keeps changing.

Research started with interviews and observation at Oro Innovations, then case studies of modular walls and workstations already on the market.

ideation

Rounds of sketching explored H-frames with rows of holes, slotting joints, pegboards, magnetic boards and toolboxes.

The name comes from the way flora grows over a landscape: serendipitously, a piece at a time, following what is around it. The system grows the same way.

concept

Under constructionStylised concept sketch: one H-frame, flip and stack

system

One H-frame, repeated. A 3 in tube with one tall and one short post, 24 by 24 in overall.

  • Flip and stack. Two short posts equal one tall post, so a flipped frame drops onto its neighbour and the lattice steps up 12 in at a time.
  • Grid. Posts sit on a 20 in square grid. Every horizontal module fills one cell.
  • No anchoring. Frames stand by interlocking, with no floor or ceiling fixings. The uneven lattice is kept on purpose to invite serendipity.

modules

One universal post clamp carries everything that hangs or sits: panels, shelves and the planter, at any height.

power

24 V DC comes in at one power-in foot and runs up through the frames, carried by concentric pogo-pin contacts that work at any rotation.

Powered attachments plug into free post tops only. Nothing powered mounts partway up a post and no cables run down the tubes. The power hub turns one post top into three USB-C outlets and a Qi pad.

colour

Colour tells you what a module does. Structure stays neutral.

02 · Design and Technology

cortX

A brain-imaging headset for elite football training

You can only improve what you measure.

Top training facilities measure almost everything about a player's body, but have nothing for kinaesthetic and spatial awareness: the sense of your own movement and of the space around you. In team ball sports, where sensory load is highest, that gap matters.

cortX is worn during training. It collects positional and neurological data so a club's analysts can work with coaches on personalised programmes.

Before refining the shell, we built a fit mock-up of the liner in translucent plastic straps and foam pads to see how it actually sits on a head.
